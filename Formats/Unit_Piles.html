Unit piles and gold total

What it shows. A pile of identical props grows per item; one prop stands for Unit Value. "What does a billion look like in gold bars?"

Actor: CK Comparison Row, Item Shape = Units.

Demo maps: L_CK_GoldStack_StudioGlass (and _Shorts), L_CK_GoldHeap_ClassicCity, L_CK_BusFleet_ClassicCity, L_CK_CoalTrains_OpenPlainDay, L_CK_PeopleCubes_AerialMall.

Key settings

Field Default What it does
Item Shape (ItemShape) Column - (Details: Comparison | Layout)
Shape Mesh (ShapeMesh) - Mesh used when a row has no Mesh of its own (Item Shape Mesh or Units). (Details: Comparison | Shape)
Unit Value (UnitValue) 1 Units: what one copy stands for (e.g. 1 bar = 12.4 kg of gold; 1 figure = 1,000,000 people). (Details: Comparison | Shape)
Unit Layout (UnitLayout) Pile - (Details: Comparison | Shape)
Unit Gap (UnitGap) 1 Units: spacing between copies, cm. (Details: Comparison | Shape)
Max Units Per Item (MaxUnitsPerItem) 20000 Units: safety cap per item (instances cost memory); raise Unit Value for bigger numbers. (Details: Comparison | Shape)
Plinth Plaque (bPlinthPlaque) true Units, Pile: the item's flag and name sit on its plinth's front as a plaque, and only the counting value rides the pile, rising with the top layer as it fills. Off: one card over the pile. Needs a Plinth Mesh and the Card label style. (Details: Comparison | Shape)
Field Aspect (FieldAspect) 0 Units, Field: the formation's width over its depth in the world (2 = twice as wide as deep), so long units (ships, jets) still form a rectangle per item: a fleet or squadron. 0 = as many columns as rows. (Details: Comparison | Shape)
Unit Lead Mesh (UnitLeadMesh) - Units, Train: stands at the front of every train (a locomotive, SM_CK_Locomotive; +X = front, pivot on the rail top). (Details: Comparison | Shape)
Unit Track Mesh (UnitTrackMesh) - Units, Train: laid end to end under the whole train, one piece every its own length (a straight track, SM_CK_RailTrack_Straight; +X along the rails, pivot on the rail top). The train rides the rails, the track's foot on the floor. (Details: Comparison | Shape)
Volume Per Unit (VolumePerUnit) 0 Mesh shape, true volume: the item's solid is value x Volume Per Unit cubic metres (gold: 1 tonne = 0.0518 m3), so heaps and blocks stand at their real size (Max Height and Scale Mode are not used). 0 = off. (Details: Comparison | Shape)
Mesh Fill (MeshFill) 1 True volume: the share of the mesh's bounding box its solid fills (a box 1, a cone pi/12 = 0.2618; SM_CK_Heap: its PILOT_NOTES). True area: the share of each picture its shape covers (an island or country cut out along its coast: pad every picture to the same share and enter it here; (Details: Comparison | Shape)
  • Shape Mesh is one unit (gold bar, coin, barrel). The count shown is value / Unit Value. If a count would exceed Max Units Per Item, raise Unit Value.
  • Unit Layout: Pile stacks layer by layer inside Column Width; Field lays the units flat as a growing square (a crowd); Train couples them end to end in one line running away from the camera, so the length of the train is the value (wagons of coal, containers on a ship).
  • Train: Unit Lead Mesh heads every line (SM_CK_Locomotive) and Unit Track Mesh tiles under it (SM_CK_RailTrack_Straight); Unit Gap is the coupling space. Model wagons along X with the wheel bottoms at z = 0.
  • Colours in a pile: the model materials (MI_CK_*, group Palette) recolour the painted parts per copy: containers and barrels pick a random colour each; the cash bundle uses one ink family for the whole pile (Palette Index 0, 1 or 2; -1 = random). Palette Amount 0 = authored colours only.
  • True volume: with Volume Per Unit the pile is sized from real volume (gold: 1 tonne = 0.0518 cubic metres).

Gold Total

After the piles, the closing shot adds a card with the combined total: set Total Label (Comparison | Overlay), for example "Combined 24,312 t". Empty means none.

Your own banknote

The cash bundle's print is a fictional currency. To show a real one, open MI_CK_CashBundle, group Face: Use Face Texture = 1 and Face Texture = your note image (156 x 66 proportion, upright). Reproducing real banknotes is regulated (for example US 31 CFR 411, ECB Decision 2013/10); check the rules for your currency before publishing.
